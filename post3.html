Punjab Boys Scouts Association (PBSA)


The Punjab Boys Scouts Association (PBSA), established in 1914, is a prominent youth development organization dedicated to building character, citizenship, and leadership skills among young people in Punjab, Pakistan. As a provincial branch of the Pakistan Boy Scouts Association, PBSA follows the principles and methods established by Lord Baden-Powell, focusing on outdoor activities, community service, and personal growth. The association serves thousands of young people across Punjab, providing structured programs that develop physical fitness, practical skills, and moral values.

PBSA is recognized for its comprehensive approach to youth development, implementing age-appropriate programs that progress from Shaheen Scouts (ages 8-11) to Rover Scouts (ages 18-25). The association organizes regular camps, hikes, community service projects, and skill-building workshops that challenge young people to discover their potential and develop self-reliance. Through its emphasis on the Scout Promise and Law, PBSA instills values of honesty, loyalty, helpfulness, and respect for others and the environment.

  • Progressive skill development through badge work and outdoor activities.
  • Leadership training through patrol system and community service.
  • Character building through adherence to Scout Promise and Law.

Beyond individual development, PBSA emphasizes community engagement and service. Scouts regularly participate in environmental conservation efforts, disaster relief operations, public health campaigns, and other initiatives that benefit society. The association promotes active citizenship, encouraging young people to understand their responsibilities to their communities and nation. Through its international connections, PBSA also provides opportunities for cultural exchange and global understanding.

The Punjab Boys Scouts Association builds character in youth and prepares them to be responsible citizens and leaders of tomorrow.
